Here's the anxiety every small business owner in Nigeria has about VAT: *Do I actually have to register?*

It's a reasonable question. VAT sounds like bureaucracy, registration sounds like headaches, and you're already juggling a dozen things. So you file it away and hope nobody asks. And then you see a mention of it in a tax update, or a customer asks about it on an invoice, and you're back to wondering: am I even obligated?

The honest answer? It depends. And after the 2025 tax reforms, the rules changed in ways that *might* mean you're off the hook. But you have to know what the actual threshold is for your business — and here's where it gets tricky: those thresholds have been reported differently in different places since the reform landed.

This guide walks you through exactly how to figure out if VAT registration applies to you, without the guesswork.

## First: What triggers VAT registration?

For years, the rule in Nigeria was simple: **if your turnover crossed a certain threshold in a 12-month period, you had to register for VAT.** Once you were over that threshold, you charged VAT on sales and paid VAT on purchases.

That basic rule hasn't changed. What *has* changed is where the threshold sits now — and who gets relief.

## The 2025 reforms created a new category: "small company"

The biggest update from the 2025 tax reforms was the creation of a formal **"small company"** category. This matters because small companies get special treatment:

- **0% company income tax** (instead of the standard 30%)
- **Modified VAT relief** (you might not have to register even if you're growing)

But here's the crucial part: **the exact definition of "small company" — and the VAT threshold — has been reported differently depending on the source.** The Nigeria Revenue Service (NRS) — the renamed FIRS as of 1 January 2026 — set these thresholds, but there's been some confusion in how they've been communicated, and in some cases, disputes about the final gazetted figures.

So rather than give you a number that might be wrong, here's the safe answer:

> **Confirm your specific VAT obligation directly with the NRS or a qualified accountant before you decide whether to register.** It takes one 15-minute conversation and removes all the guesswork.

What follows is the *framework* for thinking about it — so you know what questions to ask.

## The framework: Turnover, business type, and relief

VAT registration generally depends on three things:

### 1. Your turnover (annual revenue)

This is the primary trigger. If your business brings in revenue above a certain level, you *might* be required to register. The exact threshold is what you need to confirm with the NRS, but the concept is: bigger businesses register; genuinely small businesses might not.

### 2. Your business type

The 2025 reforms specifically mentioned that professional-services firms (lawyers, accountants, consultants) **cannot use small-company relief even if their turnover is small.** So if you're offering professional services, you might be in a different category regardless of how little you earn.

Other businesses — trading, manufacturing, logistics — have more flexibility.

### 3. Whether you qualify for small-company relief

If your business fits the small-company definition (turnover below a certain level, total fixed assets under ₦250 million, and not a professional-services firm), you might be relieved from VAT registration even if you were previously over the threshold.

## What changed in 2026: The relief got *broader*

Before the 2025 reforms, small businesses had very limited relief. Now, the relief has expanded — which means more businesses might be exempt than before.

The practical effect: **if you were VAT-registered before and your business is small, you might *now* be relieved.** But you have to confirm this with the NRS.

## The honest cost-benefit

If you *are* VAT-registered (or decide to register), here's what it costs:

**The upside:**
- You can reclaim input VAT on business purchases (goods, services, and now also capital assets as of 2026).
- You look more professional on invoices — clients know you're legitimate.
- For B2B clients, you're easier to do business with because they can reclaim their input VAT.

**The downside:**
- You have to file VAT returns monthly (even if you had no sales — a "nil" return).
- You're tracking VAT on every transaction.
- If you mess up, the NRS takes it seriously.

For a tiny business, the compliance burden might not be worth the input VAT you'd reclaim. For a growing business invoicing other businesses, VAT registration is often a net win because the clients expect it and your input VAT recovery is meaningful.

## How to actually figure out if you need to register

**Step 1: Calculate your last 12 months of turnover.**
Add up every naira of revenue from your business in the past 12 months. Include everything — sales, services, the lot. That's your annual turnover.

**Step 2: Check if your business type has restrictions.**
If you're running a professional-services business (law, accounting, consulting, etc.), note that you might not qualify for small-company relief.

**Step 3: Reach out to the NRS or a qualified accountant.**
Call the NRS (the number is on nrs.gov.ng) or email your accountant with: your business type, your annual turnover, and your total fixed assets. Ask: "Am I required to register for VAT? Do I qualify for small-company relief?"

They'll give you a straight answer.

**Step 4: If you're uncertain, over-register.**
If you're on the fence — say, right around the threshold — it's safer to register than to under-register and get a notice later. Registration is free; under-registration can cost penalties.

## If you decide you need to register (or already are)

Once you're registered, the practical job is to [calculate VAT correctly on every invoice](/blog/how-to-calculate-vat-nigeria) and [show it clearly](/blog/how-to-write-an-invoice-that-gets-paid). It's not complicated, but it's important.

You'll file monthly (even if it's a nil return). You'll track your output VAT (what you collect) and input VAT (what you pay), and remit the difference to the NRS.

## If you decide you *don't* need to register

You still need to know the rules. A customer might ask why there's no VAT on your invoice. Be ready to explain: "My business qualifies for small-company relief under the 2025 reforms, so VAT registration isn't required." That signals professionalism and knowledge, even if you're not charged VAT.

## The bottom line

VAT registration in Nigeria isn't a one-size-fits-all decision. It depends on your turnover, your business type, and whether you qualify for small-company relief under the 2025 reforms. The thresholds have changed, and the exact numbers are best confirmed directly with the NRS or a qualified accountant — a 15-minute conversation that saves you months of guesswork.

Once you *are* registered, the work is mostly routine: calculate the VAT, show it on every invoice, track what's paid, file monthly. The framework doesn't change; it's just doing it consistently and accurately.
