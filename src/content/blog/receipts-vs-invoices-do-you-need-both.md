A client pays you by transfer and says, "Please send a receipt." You've already sent them an invoice. Is that enough, or do you need a separate document?

It's one of the most common small-business paperwork questions, and the answer is simple once you see what each document actually does.

## The difference in one line

- **An invoice is a request for payment.** It says "you owe me this amount, by this date."
- **A receipt is proof of payment.** It says "you paid me this amount, on this date."

An invoice comes before the money arrives. A receipt comes after. They answer two different questions, which is why one can't always replace the other.

| | Invoice | Receipt |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Ask for payment | Confirm payment was received |
| Sent | Before payment | After payment |
| Key detail | Due date | Date paid and payment method |
| Who needs it | The client, to know what to pay | The client, as proof they paid |

## When you need an invoice

- **Payment comes after the work.** If the client pays later, an invoice sets out the amount and the due date. (Here's [how to write one that gets paid](/blog/how-to-write-an-invoice-that-gets-paid).)
- **You're billing a business.** Companies usually need an invoice to approve and process payment.
- **The job is large or ongoing,** so there has to be a record of what was agreed and charged.
- **You charge VAT.** The invoice is where the VAT is shown clearly.

## When you need a receipt

- **Any time a client asks for proof of payment.** Many businesses need it for their own records.
- **Cash sales.** When money changes hands on the spot, a receipt is often the only document.
- **Part payments and deposits.** Each payment you receive deserves a receipt showing what was paid and what remains.
- **Closing a dispute before it starts.** A receipt settles any "I already paid that" argument in seconds.

## Do you need both?

It depends on how you get paid:

**Pay-on-the-spot sales:** a receipt alone is enough. There's no separate invoice stage.

**Pay-later work:** send an invoice first, then a receipt once the money lands. This is the cleanest approach for service businesses and for anyone billing other companies.

**Deposit plus balance:** issue a receipt for the deposit, then an invoice for the balance (showing the deposit already received), then a final receipt when the balance is paid.

**Small, informal jobs:** some businesses mark the invoice "PAID" with the date and payment method once payment arrives. That can serve as a receipt, but a separate receipt is easier for clients to file and for you to track.

If your client is VAT-registered, they may need proper invoices and receipts to support their own records, so it's worth confirming what they require. Rules on documentation, and on e-invoicing, are still developing, so check with the tax authority or an accountant for your exact obligations.

## What a receipt should include

Keep it simple. A useful receipt shows:

1. The word "Receipt"
2. A unique receipt number (separate from your invoice numbers)
3. Your business name and contact details
4. The client's name
5. The date payment was received
6. The amount paid
7. The payment method (transfer, cash, POS)
8. What the payment was for, ideally referencing the invoice number
9. Any balance still owing, if it was a part payment

Matching the receipt to the invoice number it settles is the detail that makes your records clean. The same business details belong on every document, as covered in the [12-point invoice checklist](/blog/invoice-template-checklist).

## Common mistakes

- **Calling an invoice a receipt.** An invoice doesn't prove anything has been paid.
- **Issuing a receipt before the money has actually arrived.** Wait until the payment is confirmed in your account.
- **No numbering.** Unnumbered receipts are hard to track and easy to lose.
- **Skipping receipts for part payments.** These are where disputes begin.
- **Not keeping copies.** Save every receipt, ideally in a folder by month.

## How it fits your bookkeeping

Invoices tell you what you're owed. Receipts confirm what you've actually been paid. Together they give you a clean record of money in, and they make your monthly check much easier.
