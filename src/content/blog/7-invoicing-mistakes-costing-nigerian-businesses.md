You've invoiced hundreds of clients over the years, and you'd think by now you'd have it down to a science. But even seasoned business owners make invoicing mistakes — small ones that seem harmless, until you add them up over months and realise they've cost you thousands in lost revenue, uncollected money, and wasted chasing time.

This guide covers the seven mistakes that trip up Nigerian small business owners most often. Catch yourself on any of these, and you've just found money you're leaving on the table.

## 1. No invoice number (or a different number format every time)

**The mistake:** Sending invoices titled "Invoice 1," "Invoice 2," then switching to "INV-001" later, or using the date instead of a number. Or worse: no number at all, just "Invoice to John."

**Why it costs you:**
- You can't track invoices cleanly — your records are a mess
- When you follow up, you can't reference it precisely ("Can you check on the invoice I sent last week?")
- It looks disorganised, which makes clients less likely to pay promptly
- You can't spot duplicates or voided invoices easily

**The fix:** Start with INV-001 and go sequentially. Every invoice gets a unique number. It takes 30 seconds to be consistent forever after that.

## 2. No due date (or a vague one like "ASAP" or "Upon Receipt")

**The mistake:** Sending an invoice with no stated due date, or writing "Payment due ASAP" or "Due on delivery."

**Why it costs you:**
- No due date = no deadline in the client's mind = indefinite delay
- "Upon receipt" is vague — they don't know exactly when they received it
- You have no firm ground to stand on if you need to follow up later
- Late-payment excuses become: "I didn't realise it was due yet"

**The fix:** A specific date. "Due: 30 September 2026." Full stop. It creates a psychological anchor and gives you something firm to reference.

## 3. Vague line items ("Services rendered — ₦200,000")

**The mistake:** Lumping everything into one line with a generic description.

**Why it costs you:**
- Client sees a big number with no justification and hesitates
- Opens the door to negotiation: "Why ₦200,000 exactly?"
- If there's a dispute, you have nothing to fall back on
- Client can't explain the invoice to their finance person, which stalls approval

**The fix:** Break it down. "Website redesign (3 weeks, 40 hours @ ₦1,500/hr) — ₦60,000. Logo design (5 concepts) — ₦30,000..." Specific line items make it real and defensible.

## 4. Missing or buried bank details

**The mistake:** Putting your account number in tiny text at the bottom, or omitting it entirely and forcing the client to ask.

**Why it costs you:**
- Every extra step you make the client take = days of delay
- If they have to email asking for your details, that's a 24-hour round-trip you didn't need
- Some clients just give up and pay late or not at all rather than chase you
- It looks unprofessional

**The fix:** Make it bold and obvious. "Payment details: [Bank] Account [Number] in the name of [Your Business Name]" — centre it, make it stand out. If you accept multiple methods (transfer, payment link), list them all.

## 5. Hiding or inconsistent VAT (when you charge it)

**The mistake:** Either not showing VAT separately, or calculating it wrong, or showing it inconsistently across invoices.

**Why it costs you:**
- If VAT isn't shown as a line item, your business customers can't reclaim their input VAT — which makes them less likely to buy from you next time (they have to absorb an extra cost)
- Wrong calculations: you either undersell yourself or look careless
- Inconsistency raises questions: "Did you charge VAT last time? Why not this time?"

**The fix:** [Calculate VAT correctly](/blog/how-to-calculate-vat-nigeria) and show it as its own line every time. "Subtotal — ₦150,000. VAT (7.5%) — ₦11,250. Total — ₦161,250." Clean, consistent, professional.

## 6. No payment terms or late-fee policy stated

**The mistake:** Sending an invoice with no explanation of what happens if payment is late.

**Why it costs you:**
- Client thinks late payment is consequence-free
- If you do charge a late fee, they feel blindsided
- You have no professional footing to charge it after the fact
- It signals that timing doesn't matter to you

**The fix:** State your terms clearly. "Payment due within 7 days of invoice date. Late payments incur a 2% monthly charge." Be firm, be clear, no surprises.

## 7. Invoicing long after the work is done

**The mistake:** Finishing a project, then waiting a week, two weeks, or even a month to send the invoice.

**Why it costs you:**
- The work fades from the client's mind — they forget the urgency
- It signals that you don't value payment or tracking
- Payment clock starts later, so it's paid even later
- You look disorganised

**The fix:** Invoice the moment work is complete. Same day if possible. It signals professionalism and starts the payment clock immediately.

## The quiet cost of these mistakes

Each one alone might cost you a few days or a few hundred naira. But add them up over a year — 20 clients, one mistake per invoice, each one delaying payment by an average of 5 days — and you're looking at ₦50,000–₦200,000 in cash you're not collecting on time, or not collecting at all.

That's money your business needs *now*, not three months from now.
